## **Can you retire in Thailand on Social Security?**

<p class="lead-quote">Yes, if your check is near average. $2,071 covers a lean budget in all four cities with $920 to $1,181 left, and a comfortable one with $452 to $820 left.
The catch is the visa: the income route needs 65,000 baht ($1,944) a month, or else 800,000 baht ($23,931) in a Thai bank. 
At home, the same basket plus Medicare Part B costs $2,727, a $656 monthly gap, or about $39,400 of savings every 5 years.</p>

**How we compared:**

* One retiree, age 61 to 70, renting a 1 bedroom alone.
* Checks: the 2026 average ($2,071) and the median ($1,992, worked out from [SSA Table 5.B6](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/statcomps/supplement/2026/5b.html)).
* Thai budgets: Numbeo's single person costs without rent, plus rent, health insurance and visa fees.
* US: the same Numbeo basket, national figure, plus Medicare Part B in the savings section.
* Rate: 33.43 baht per dollar ([Federal Reserve H.10](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h10/current/), 16 Oct 2026). Numbeo prices read 22 Oct 2026.

## **A deeper look**

Only if your check is at least 65,000 baht, about $1,944 a month. The average and median pass; women's average and the bottom quarter don't.

| Check (SSA)                | In baht | Vs 65,000 baht | Income route? |
| -------------------------- | ------- | -------------- | ------------- |
| Men's average, $2,282.28   | 76,297  | +17.4%         | Yes           |
| Average, $2,071            | 69,234  | +6.5%          | Yes           |
| Median, $1,992             | 66,593  | +2.5%          | Yes, just     |
| Women's average, $1,872.43 | 62,595  | minus 3.7%     | No            |
| 25th percentile, $1,366.31 | 45,676  | minus 29.7%    | No            |

Sources: [SSA 2026 COLA fact sheet](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html) · [SSA Table 5.B6](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/statcomps/supplement/2026/5b.html) · [Federal Reserve H.10](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h10/current/)

* 48% of retired workers get less than $1,944 a month ([SSA Table 5.B6](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/statcomps/supplement/2026/5b.html), December 2025).
* Keep Medicare Part B and [$202.90 a month](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles) comes out first, so the average check lands at $1,868 (62,451 baht), under the line.
* The US Embassy [stopped issuing income letters in 2019](https://www.thephuketnews.com/us-embassy-in-thailand-to-cease-providing-income-affidavit-69127.php), so the income route means 12 months of deposits into a Thai bank. Social Security can pay straight into a Thai account ([SSA direct deposit list](https://www.ssa.gov/international/countrylist6.htm)).
* ![Monthly checks](/blog/images/54.2.png)

**Sources:** SSA 2026 COLA fact sheet, SSA Table 5.B6, SSA International Direct Deposit list, Phuket News on the US Embassy notice.

| O-A (long stay, from the US)                           | 800,000 baht, or 65,000 baht a month, or a mix; online version $30,000 or $2,500 a month                                                                           | 3,000,000 baht ($89,740) or $100,000 cover                                               | 1 year; $200                                                                      |
| O-X (10 years)                                         | 3,000,000 baht ($89,740) deposit, or 1,800,000 baht plus 1,200,000 baht a year income                                                                              | IPD 400,000 and OPD 40,000 baht                                                          | 5 plus 5 years; $400                                                              |
| LTR Wealthy Pensioner                                  | $80,000 a year passive income, or $40,000 plus $250,000 invested                                                                                                   | $50,000 cover or $100,000 in a bank                                                      | 10 years; 50,000 baht ($1,496)                                                    |

* The deposit has to sit in a Thai bank 2 months before and 3 months after approval, then never drop below 400,000 baht ($11,965).
* The LTR income test is 3.2 times the average yearly check, so it is out of reach for most Social Security retirees.
* Soft wording: O-X figures come from a consulate page dated 22 Sep 2023 and the extension rules from 2023. Write "as listed by the Thai consulate in Chicago" and add "check the current rules with the embassy before you apply".
* Note the 2026 change: from 15 Sep 2026 US passport holders get 30 days visa free (was 60), so plan the visa before you fly.

**Sources:** Royal Thai Embassy DC (Non-O, 1 Jul 2026; O-A, 1 Sep 2026), Thai Consulate Chicago, Thailand.go.th, Visathailand.net, BOI LTR, Siam Legal, Royal Thai Embassy Moscow notice.

### **What's left after the bills?**

Left over is money not yet spent, not savings. It still has to cover flights home, dental work, the 100,000 baht ($2,991) deductible on the cheap insurance plan and anything Numbeo's basket leaves out.

## **How much savings do you need on top of your check?**

In Thailand, none on an average check. In the US, the same check needs about $39,400 in the bank for every 5 years. And you can't top up with a job: of the O-A visa, the Royal Thai Embassy in Washington says ["Employment of any kind is strictly prohibited."](https://washingtondc.thaiembassy.org/en/page/non-oa) So any monthly gap comes out of savings.

Both sides carry a health cost here: the Thai budgets include insurance, and the US adds Medicare Part B ([$202.90 a month](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles)).

## **Do you pay tax in Thailand on Social Security, 401(k) or IRA money?**

Not on Social Security: the treaty says only the US can tax it. 401(k) and IRA money may be taxed when you bring it in.

| Income                               | Thai tax?                                                              | US tax?                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Social Security                      | No, taxable only in the US (treaty Article 20(2))                      | Up to 50% taxable above $25,000 single / $32,000 joint, up to 85% above $34,000 / $44,000 |
| 401(k), IRA, private pension         | Likely, if a tax resident (180+ days) brings in money earned from 2024 | Yes, the US still taxes citizens                                                          |
| US government pension                | No, unless you're a Thai national                                      | Yes                                                                                       |
| Any foreign income, LTR visa holders | Exempt when brought in                                                 | Yes                                                                                       |

Sources: [US-Thailand treaty](https://www.rd.go.th/fileadmin/download/nation/america_e.pdf) · [IRS Publication 915](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p915) · [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/tax/revenue-department-s-guidance-on-foreign-income) · [Royal Decree 743](https://ltr.boi.go.th/documents/Royal%20Decree%20issued%20under%20the%20Revenue%20Code%20No.743%20%28EN%29.pdf)

* The 401(k) and IRA line is our reading of the treaty. No IRS or Revenue Department page confirms it, so check with a tax pro who knows both systems.
* Example: bring in $1,000 a month of IRA money and Thai tax is about 11,616 baht ($347) a year. If the [190,000 baht allowance for over 65s](https://www.forvismazars.com/th/en/insights/doing-business-in-thailand/payroll/personal-income-tax) applies, it drops to $2, but it's not confirmed foreigners get it.
* Income earned before 2024 is protected. A proposed exemption is still a draft, [not law as of 19 Oct 2026](https://www.thailawonline.com/thailand-remittance-tax-exemption-status/).
* US side: 12 average checks add up to $24,852, so a single filer can have about $12,574 of other income before any benefit is taxable. The 2026 standard deduction is [$16,100](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill), plus [$6,000](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/check-your-eligibility-for-the-new-enhanced-deduction-for-seniors) for 65+.
* The 800,000 baht deposit ($23,931) is over the $10,000 [FBAR](https://www.fincen.gov/report-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts) line, so you'd file one every year.

## **The verdict**

Retiring in Thailand on Social Security is worth it if your check is average or higher, you can get insured and yearly visa paperwork doesn't bother you. It turns a $656 monthly shortfall at home into $920 to $1,181 spare on a lean budget.

Think twice if your check is under $1,944 and you don't have $23,931 to park in a Thai bank, if you need care only Medicare covers, or if a 9% currency swing would break your budget. Check how your IRA or 401(k) money will be taxed before you move.
